Keystone design system

Primitives, Theme, Surface

Every colour in Keystone passes through three layers. Each one answers a single question, and components only ever see the last one. Pick a brand to follow one colour through all three.

The big picture

One card, two grounds. The theme gives every role a primitive, the surface decides which roles apply on this ground, and the card only ever asks for core and content-primary. On Inverse the same primitives simply swap jobs.

One component, four grounds

The same card, with no colour of its own, placed on each surface for the selected brand. The labels show the primitive each role lands on. Subtle only swaps the background and borders; Inverse and Brand re-point the text and actions too. A fifth mode, Dashboard body, is the grey page behind dashboard cards and works like Subtle.

Every brand on every ground

Rows are themes, columns are surfaces. All twenty cards are the same component with no colour of its own. That is why Theme and Surface are separate: in one collection this would need a mode for every brand and ground (10 themes × 5 surfaces = 50), and every new client would add five more. Kept apart, a new client adds two Theme modes and Surface never changes.

Adding a new client

The brief stays small. The system does the rest.

  1. The client gives five or six coloursPrimary, secondary, accent, maybe a neutral. Nothing else.
  2. Each colour becomes a scaleA family in the client's own namespace, stepped by lightness: client/blue/100 to client/blue/900.
  3. Map the rolesPoint the new Theme mode's roles at primitives: core palette for status and neutrals, the client's own family for brand roles.
  4. Run the contrast checkEvery text role at 4.5:1 and every border, focus ring and chart series at 3:1, on every surface.
  5. ShipNo component changes. Every screen in every app picks up the new brand.
